# Limitations of corona3d

corona3d reconstructs the corona, a coronal mass ejection (CME), and the sources of its radio burst in three dimensions for any event, and the same steps are applied whether the event is the validation case of 2013 October 25 or a new one. The reconstruction combines a model of the corona before the CME, a geometrical shell fitted to white-light images, and radio positions that are measured in the plane of the sky. Each of these inputs has a known range of validity, and this document states where that range ends so that a result can be judged before it is used in a publication. The methods themselves are described in [METHODS.md](METHODS.md), and `corona3d event` writes the limitations that apply to one event into its `event_report.md`.

## 1. Halo CMEs and the direction of the shell

The free fit of the six parameters of the graduated cylindrical shell (GCS; Thernisien et al. 2006; Thernisien 2011) needs a CME whose envelope has a well-defined outline in at least two viewpoints. A halo CME, which is directed nearly along the line of sight of the observer, appears as a ring around the occulter in that viewpoint, and the fit then constrains the height and the direction only weakly. Moreover, the front of such a CME cannot be measured in the plane of the sky of that instrument, and the extrapolation of the apex height to the earlier time (Section 7.2 of METHODS.md) relies on a limb view. In these cases, when the free fit is poor even after a view that it cannot match has been left out, `corona3d event` falls back to the fit with the direction held at the flare site if that fit is the better one, and the shell then has the direction that the user supplied and not one that was measured. The report names the fallback, and the fit quality is stated with the intersection over union (IoU) in each instrument. When the fit has only one view left, or when its views are less than 30° apart in observer position, the direction is not free at all: following Thernisien (2011) it is bounded within `--direction-cone` (default 15°) of the flare site, or fixed there with 0, and the report states "direction constrained by the source region (single viewpoint)". The direction of such a shell is therefore assumed from the flare and not measured. On 2024 May 9, before this rule was introduced, the free fit on COR1-A alone placed the axis at latitude +14° for a flare at S14.

## 2. Gaps in the data

The white-light images of an instrument may be absent for an interval, as happened for the C2 coronagraph of the Large Angle and Spectrometric Coronagraph (LASCO) on 2024 May 9, when no frame exists between 14:48 and 18:52 UT. A rendering with a `wl_time` inside such a gap is refused when the nearest frame is more than 30 min away, and the error and the report give the nearest frame times before and after it. In `corona3d event`, the instrument is then left out of the coronagraph figure, and when no instrument has a usable frame, `wl_time` moves to the nearest frame that can be drawn and the report states it. In every case, `wl_time` follows the time of the fit, which `--t-ref` sets or the program chooses from the images. A gap also removes frames from the height-time profile of the front, so that fewer than two usable fronts may remain and the extrapolation cannot be made. In that case the apex height at the time of the reconstruction has to be given by hand with `--height-out` and `--height-note`, and it is recorded as assumed.

## 3. Small separation between Earth and the STEREO spacecraft

The shell is fitted to images from more than one viewpoint, and the direction of a CME is well constrained only when these viewpoints are separated by a sufficiently large angle. In 2013 the separation of Earth and STEREO-B was 142°, whereas STEREO-A and Earth were separated by only about 12° in Carrington longitude on 2024 May 9 (317.2° and 329.6°), so that the C2 and COR images then see the CME nearly from the same side. The direction obtained from such a pair of viewpoints is degenerate along the common line of sight, and the direction is therefore bounded around the flare site (`--direction-cone`) when the observers are less than 30° apart.

The apex height of the shell is also limited by the field of view. A coronagraph does not show a front beyond its outer edge, so that the fit bounds the apex height at 1.5 times the largest outer radius of the fitted views (6 R⊙ for COR1 alone, 9 R⊙ with C2, at most 15 R⊙) and judges a fit poor when its apex lies outside every fitted field or its half-angle is below 5°. A fit that ends at this bound has not measured the height, and a CME whose front has left the field of the only available instrument cannot be fitted reliably from it; a later frame of a wider-field coronagraph is then needed.

## 4. Assumed heights

Where the front cannot be measured at the time of the reconstruction, the apex height is an assumption. The height extrapolated from a measured front assumes a constant speed between the measured frames and the reconstruction time, and an accelerating CME moves more slowly early in its rise, so that the true height may be lower. A height given by hand with `--height-out` is a kinematic estimate of the user, which corona3d records in `gcs_fit` (`"height_source": "assumed"`) and lists under the assumptions of the report.

## 5. Radio sources and the manual step for the NRH

The 2-D images of the Nançay Radioheliograph (NRH) cannot be downloaded automatically. The BASS2000 archive provides only four snapshots per day at 164 and 327 MHz, movies, and visibility files, which can be imaged only with the NRH routines of the IDL SolarSoft package, and the 10 s images are delivered by the Radio Solar Database at Nançay (https://rsdb.obs-nancay.fr/) after a request. The user must therefore request the images (Stokes I, and Stokes V for the polarisation) for the frequencies and the interval of the event and copy them into `data/nrh/`, after which `corona3d fetch --nrh` reports which frequencies the files cover. The images of the Owens Valley Radio Observatory Long Wavelength Array (OVRO-LWA), in contrast, are public from 2024 April onward and are downloaded by `corona3d fetch --ovro-lwa`.

The radio positions are placed at the heights of the four-fold Newkirk model for emission at the second harmonic, on the side of the plane of the sky that faces the observer. Both choices are assumptions. The true height depends on the density along the line of sight, on the harmonic, and on the scattering of the radio waves, which displaces observed sources outward and is not corrected, and a source cannot be placed at its height when its sky-plane position lies farther from Sun centre than that height. The centroid of a source has an uncertainty of about one pixel of the image.

## 6. Resolution and age of the coronal model

The MAS/MAST run of Predictive Science Inc. (PSI) is a steady-state magnetohydrodynamic model of the corona on a mesh of finite resolution, computed for one Carrington rotation from the magnetogram of one date. It does not contain the CME, so that the field lines and the density surface show the corona that the CME meets, without its deformation, and it may differ from the real corona at the time of the event. Structures smaller than a few cells of the mesh are not resolved, and the field lines, the density surface, and the Alfvén speed depend on the resolution of the run chosen with `run`. For the validation event of 2013 October 25, the run that Morosan et al. (2022) used is no longer served by PSI, and the public run agrees with their density surface in outline but not in every detail. The density scale of 2 is the value used by Morosan et al. (2022), and it is not derived from the model.

## 7. Coronagraph images

The coronagraph images are level 0.5 and are not calibrated photometrically, so that the ratio or difference against the first frame of the window is a relative change that is suited to the comparison of positions and not to estimates of brightness or mass. Each image is drawn in the plane of the sky of its own spacecraft, which is exact only when seen from that spacecraft; seen from another viewpoint, the plane shows only where the image lies and not the three-dimensional structure of the CME.

## 8. Shock diagnostics

The Mach numbers of `corona3d shock` rest on three assumptions, each of which can change them by tens of per cent or more. First, the GCS shell stands for the shock surface, without a stand-off distance, so that a shock that runs ahead of the CME front meets the plasma earlier and farther out than the shell. Secondly, the speed of every point other than the apex follows from the self-similar expansion of the shell at the apex speed of the height-time fit, which is measured in white light minutes after the burst and assumed constant; a CME that expands laterally faster than self-similarly early in its rise, as is often observed, has flank speeds and flank Mach numbers above those computed. Thirdly, the upstream corona is the steady MAS/MAST run of the Carrington rotation (Section 6), whose streamer and Alfvén speeds can be displaced from those of the day, and the Alfvén Mach number changes by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ between the two choices of `va_density`. Where the Alfvén speed of the run falls well below the sound speed, as at some points of closed field, the Alfvén Mach number becomes large while the fast-magnetosonic Mach number stays moderate, and the latter is the measure of the shock strength. The radio sources are placed at one model height, so that their distance from the shell, and whether they lie ahead of or behind it, change within the height range of Section 13 of METHODS.md. The EUV height uses the off-limb part of the EUV envelope only and keeps the shape of the white-light fit, and the degree of circular polarisation depends on the calibration of the NRH Stokes V images, which at some frequencies hold artefacts larger than the source.
