# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The README, the documentation, the citation file, and the module docstrings describe corona3d with what it adds to earlier three-dimensional reconstructions: the event of 2013 October 25 is the validation case, compared with the reconstruction of Morosan et al. (2022), the user guide has a section on what is new and a table that compares the two reconstructions item by item (Section 11), and every number, command, option, and figure is unchanged.
- Every layer of the figure can be made semitransparent through the new configuration key `opacity` and the option `corona3d render --opacity LAYER=VALUE`. The radio contours, the radio blobs, the density surface, and the coronagraph images also take a variable opacity: one value per contour level (from the lowest to the highest) or per blob (from the first image to the last), and for the surface and the coronagraph images a list or a transfer function such as `linear` over the colour range, so that the dark background of a ratio image can be transparent while the CME front stays opaque. The radio contours can be drawn at several fractions of the peak with `contour_levels` in an entry of `radio_images` or `--contour-levels 0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9` (which implies the new `--contours`), and they are semitransparent by default (0.6). `wl_opacity` remains valid and replaces `opacity.wl`. The defaults of all other layers are the former fixed values, and the three 2013 figures are unchanged pixel for pixel (`reconstruction.check_opacity`, `reconstruction.opacities`; tests in `tests/test_opacity.py` and `tests/test_nrh.py`).
- The CME envelopes of `corona3d gcs` are measured more robustly: saturated and stray-light pixels of the base frame are masked (`gcs.clean_diff`), and the difference is smoothed with a Gaussian of 0.06 R_sun, opened with a radius of 0.05 R_sun, and cleared of bright regions below 0.1 R_sun^2 before the convex hull. A repeated 2013 fit changes little (mean IoU 0.825 instead of 0.804, 1.83 instead of 1.94 R_sun at 15:00 UT, within its uncertainty); on 2024 May 9 the COR1-A fit rises from IoU 0.59 to 0.89 at 17:51 UT with the axis in the south, but its apex of 5.1 R_sun is still outside the field. An instrument without frames in the folder is now left out of the fit with a message instead of stopping it.
- `corona3d render --shell-on-top` draws the GCS shell and the radio sources in a second layer over the model surface, because a shell at the burst time (1.77 R_sun on 2024 May 9) lies largely inside the surface of constant density and was hidden by it. Off by default; the 2013 figure is unchanged.
- The coronagraph figure can be drawn at the burst time: `corona3d render --wl-at burst` and `corona3d event --wl-at burst` place the shell at the reconstruction time with the parameters at t_out (a height that is assumed stays marked as assumed on the panel and in `event_report.md`), and each coronagraph shows its own frame nearest to that time after its base frame. A frame more than 30 min away is labelled as the nearest frame with its distance. A configuration without `gcs_fit` takes `--wl-height` and `--wl-height-note`. The default (`--wl-at fit`) and the 2013 figure are unchanged (`reconstruction.burst_shell`, `whitelight.nearest_frame`, `load_plane(nearest=True)`). Reason: for 2024 May 9 the shell was drawn at 18:52 UT, 80 min after the OVRO-LWA type II sources of 17:32 to 17:37 UT.
- The automatic fit time t_ref is chosen while the CME front is still inside the field of view (`gcs.FRONT_FRAC` 0.9): the front, measured from the envelope as in `front_profile`, must lie at most 0.9 of the outer field radius from the Sun in the view, together with the developed-envelope criterion (3 R_sun^2). Among the times at which the largest number of views satisfy this, the earliest is taken. When none does, the earlier rule is kept and `event_report.md` says so; `t_ref_choice` records `reason`, `front_fraction` and `front_inside_fov`. An explicit `--t-ref` is unchanged. `gcs.pick_t_ref` now returns the time, the views and the rule that applied. Reason: on 2024 May 9 the former choice, 18:06 UT, lay after the front had left the 4 R_sun field of COR1-A. METHODS 7.2, USER_GUIDE and offline tests in `tests/test_event.py`.
- The apex height of every GCS fit is bounded above by 1.5 times the largest outer field-of-view radius of the fitted views (`gcs.HEIGHT_FOV_FACTOR`, `gcs.height_limit`; 6 R_sun for COR1 alone, 9 R_sun with C2, never above 15 R_sun), for the fit to all views, every refit and single-view fit of the view rule, and the fixed-direction fit. `gcs.quality` marks a fit poor when the half-angle is below 5 deg (`gcs.ALPHA_MIN`, a degenerate shell) or when the apex lies outside the outer field of view of every fitted view, and uses the bounds of the fit, so that a height at the new upper bound is a bound hit. The bound is recorded in `height_bound` of `gcs_fit.json` and named in `event_report.md`. Reason: on 2024 May 9 the fit to COR1-A alone, which sees out to 4 R_sun, had an apex at 14.6 R_sun and a half-angle of 1.7 deg, a thin shell whose legs matched the envelope.
- The view rule no longer lets the joint fit choose the surviving view when the observers are less than 30 deg apart (`gcs.check_views` with `limit` and `problems`). On 2024 May 9 both views are from STEREO-A, the joint fit gave COR1-A 0.28 and COR2-A 0.62, COR1-A was dropped, and the kept COR2-A fit had the aspect ratio at its bound of 0.1 (alpha 59.6 deg near its bound of 60 deg), whereas COR1-A alone reaches 0.90. Each view (or each subset with one view left out) is now fitted separately and the refits are compared. A fit with no parameter at a bound comes first, then the higher mean IoU, and within 0.02 the fit with more views and the view nearer the Sun. All candidates are recorded in `refits` of `view_check` (with `bounds`) and described in one sentence in the report. Widely separated views keep the former behaviour. When the kept fit still has kappa or alpha at a bound, `event_report.md` names the parameter and the fit stays poor, and the bounds are unchanged. METHODS 7.3 and USER_GUIDE are updated, and offline tests with synthetic masks and callbacks are in `tests/test_gcs_fit.py`.
- The direction of the GCS shell is no longer free when it cannot be measured: with one view left in the fit, or views whose observers are less than 30 deg apart (`gcs.direction_limit`), the longitude and latitude are bounded within `--direction-cone DEG` of the flare site (default 15; 0 fixes them), the practice of Thernisien (2011). On 2024 May 9 the free fit on COR1-A alone (IoU 0.902) had put the axis at latitude +14 deg for a flare at S14. The constraint is recorded in `direction` of `gcs_fit.json`, in `direction_constraint` of `gcs_fit` and in the report ("direction constrained by the source region (single viewpoint)"); a direction at the edge of the cone is not counted as a bound hit in the quality check. The 2013 three-view fit stays free. METHODS 7.4 (the later sections move down by one), USER_GUIDE, and LIMITATIONS; offline tests in `tests/test_gcs_fit.py`.

- Order of the fit decision in `corona3d event --fit auto`: the free fit on all views, the view rule and a refit of the free fit, the judgement of its quality, the fixed-direction fit (also with the view rule) only when the free fit is poor or failed, and the better of the two (a good fit beats a poor one, then the higher mean IoU of the kept views, a difference below 0.02 to the free fit; `gcs.choose_fit`). The view rule kept a refit only when the mean IoU of the other views rose by 0.05, so that on 2024 May 9 COR2-A with an IoU of 0 stayed in the free fit (COR1-A 0.9009 with it, 0.9017 without it), the mean of 0.45 counted as poor, and the fixed-direction fit with a mean of 0.38 replaced it; the refit is now kept unless the mean IoU of the other views falls by more than 0.01. The steps, both fits, and the criterion are written to `decision` in `gcs_fit.json` and to *Fit quality* in `event_report.md`; the list of fitted views comes from the views used (`views_used`), so that an instrument without an envelope, such as C2 when its first frame is the base frame, is never listed. METHODS 7.3 and 7.4, USER_GUIDE and LIMITATIONS updated; offline tests on the May 9 numbers.
- `corona3d event` and `corona3d gcs` choose the time of the six-parameter fit from the images when `--t-ref` is not given: the earliest coronagraph frame within 150 min after the reconstruction time at which the envelope covers at least 3 R_sun^2 with its front 0.2 R_sun inside the field edge in most views (`gcs.pick_t_ref`, `gcs.auto_t_ref`). Before, the default was the reconstruction time plus 60 min, which for the type II burst of 2024 May 9 (17:32 UT) gave a fit at 18:32 UT. The reconstruction time (shell, radio interval) is always `--time`, the begin time of the burst with `--burst`. The choice is recorded in `gcs_fit` (`t_ref_choice`) and stated in `event_report.md`; `wl_time` follows the time of the fit (`wl_time_from` is `"t_ref chosen automatically"`).
- White-light figure of `corona3d event`: an instrument whose nearest frame to `wl_time` is the base frame (the first frame of `wl_window`, as LASCO C2 at 18:52 UT on 2024 May 9) is now a gap like one without a frame within 30 min, which is why the figure failed although a frame lay within the tolerance (`pipeline.wl_gaps`). When no instrument has a usable frame, `wl_time` moves to the time of the nearest frame that can be drawn (`pipeline.wl_nearest`), the figure is drawn with it, and the report says so under Warnings.
- Exit code of `corona3d event`: 0 for a partial result, with the failed steps under a new section Warnings of `event_report.md`; 1 only when no figure could be drawn. `--strict` restores the former behaviour (1 when any step failed).
- `pip install corona3d` is prepared (not uploaded to PyPI). The metadata in `pyproject.toml` has classifiers, the licence file, URLs, and lower bounds of the dependencies; the core install is light (NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, Astropy, SunPy, pyhdf, platformdirs) and the extras are `render` (PyVista, PyThea), `gcs` (PyThea, scikit-image), `radio` (h5py), `all`, `docs`, and `test` (the former extra `fit` is now `gcs`). PyVista, PyThea, scikit-image, and h5py are imported where they are first used, and a missing one stops with the extra to install (`corona3d/_extras.py`). The wheel (70 kB) holds only the package and its data (`default_config.json`, `events/*.json`); the sdist adds the documentation text, the tests, and the licence. `docs/RELEASING.md` lists the release steps.
- Nothing depends on the repository layout: when neither `--home`, `CORONA3D_HOME`, nor a `config.json` in the current folder is found, the working directory is the platform cache directory (platformdirs). `corona3d --version` and the new `corona3d doctor [--offline]` (`corona3d/doctor.py`) report the installed extras, the working directory and its origin, the package data, and whether PSI, the VSO, the OVRO-LWA bucket, NOAA NCEI, and JPL Horizons answer. Offline tests in `tests/test_packaging.py`.
- The base frame of the GCS envelopes no longer depends on the start of the download: for each instrument and observing programme it is the last frame at or before the flare onset (`--onset`, default the time of the reconstruction) minus `--base-minutes` (default 10 min), and the earliest frame only when none is that early (reported). The onset, the cutoff, the base frames, and the fallbacks are recorded in `base` of `gcs_fit` in `config.json` and in `gcs_fit.json`. METHODS Section 7.2 and the user guide describe the rule, and `tests/test_gcs_fit.py` tests it on synthetic frame lists. This settles the open question on the 2013 October 25 height (1.80 against 1.93 R⊙ for two download windows).
- A view whose IoU is far below the others no longer spoils the GCS fit: after the first fit, `corona3d gcs` and `corona3d event` take a view with an IoU below 0.5 (`--iou-min`) and below 0.75 times the mean of the others, fit again without it, and keep the refit when the mean IoU of the remaining views rises by at least 0.05; views are removed one at a time, the worst first, and at least one remains. Both fits are written to `view_check` in `gcs_fit.json` and to the section *Fit quality* of `event_report.md`, and the views left out to `views_dropped` in `gcs_fit` of `config.json`. The option `--drop-poor-views` (default) or `--keep-all-views` switches the rule; the rule is in `docs/METHODS.md`, Section 7.3 (`gcs.poor_view`, `gcs.check_views`, offline tests on synthetic masks in `tests/test_gcs_fit.py`).
- `corona3d event --time DATE` with a date and no time lists the type II and type III bursts of that day from the daily edited events of NOAA SWPC (served without a login by the NCEI archive, with the SWPC FTP server as fallback), and `--burst N` runs the event at the begin time of burst N, for example `--time 2024-05-09 --burst 4` for the type II burst of 17:32 UT. The file is cached in `data/bursts/`. An unreachable source or a day without bursts is reported and asks for `--time` (`corona3d/bursts.py`, `tests/test_bursts.py` with a stored file of 2013 October 25).
- `corona3d fetch` ends with a message that names the number of failed files per source and the remedy (the same command again), instead of a bare exit code 1; the failed Fido downloads are printed one by one. The intermittent exit code 1 of the online test in 2026 October could not be reproduced (the next run passed); the only place where `corona3d fetch` exits with 1 is a failed or damaged download, which this message now names.
- The envelope areas of the COR1-B frames of 2013 October 25 differ from `scripts/gcs_fit_2013-10-25.json` (2.16 against 2.66 R⊙² at 15:08) because the base frame was then the first frame of the folder (replaced by the fixed pre-event window above): the folder of the stored fit starts at 14:43 UT and the current download at 14:35 UT. The old and the current code give identical values on the same folder; documented in `docs/METHODS.md`, Section 7.2.

- The height-time step of `corona3d gcs` and `corona3d event` uses only the uninterrupted run of frames whose envelope covers at least 2 R⊙² (before: 1 R⊙², with gaps allowed). On the real data of 2013 October 25 the small envelopes of 1.0 and 1.2 R⊙² before the eruption had flattened the line to 334 km s⁻¹ and 2.71 R⊙ at 15:00 UT; the run of four frames gives 1126 km s⁻¹ and 1.80 ± 0.06 R⊙ (`gcs.front_profile`, `tests/test_gcs_fit.py`).
- `corona3d event` draws the coronagraph figure with the instruments that have a frame within 30 min of `wl_time` and names the others as missing in `event_report.md`; before, a gap of one instrument (LASCO C2 on 2024 May 9) failed the whole figure (`pipeline.wl_gaps`).
- `corona3d event --t-ref` and `corona3d gcs --t-ref` also set `wl_time` of `config.json` to the time of the fit, so that the coronagraph figure is drawn at that time when the images have a gap (for 2024 May 9, LASCO C2 has no frame between 14:48 and 18:52 UT, and `--t-ref 2024-05-09T17:55` gives `wl_time` 17:55). The provenance `gcs_fit` records the value as `wl_time` with `"wl_time_from": "--t-ref"` (`corona3d/gcs.py`, `corona3d/pipeline.py`).
- `--views` of `corona3d gcs` and `corona3d event` and `--view` of `corona3d render` accept the legacy names `stereo_a` and `stereo_b`, as `paths.legacy` does for the configuration. In `--views` they stand for COR1 and COR2 of that spacecraft, and in `--view` for the view from `stereo`; a name for the spacecraft that is not `stereo_sc` of the event is refused.
- When a coronagraph has no frame within `wl_window`, or none within 30 min of `wl_time`, the error of the rendering and `event_report.md` name the nearest frame times before and after `wl_time` (`corona3d/whitelight.py`).
- Documentation for solar physicists: the README (purpose, installation, the one-command quick start, what is measured and what is assumed, data sources with their access, citation, and licence), the restructured user guide, `docs/METHODS.md` (moved from the repository root, with equations for the MAS/MAST units and the Alfvén speed, the Runge-Kutta tracing of field lines, the four-fold Newkirk heights, the GCS geometry and the fit with its IoU and quality criteria, the coordinate frames, and the plane-of-sky projection), and the new `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`. The references Mikić et al. (1999), Thernisien et al. (2006), and Chhabra et al. (2021) are added to `docs/refs.bib`.
- `corona3d event --time T --flare S14W28` runs `new`, `fetch`, `radio-interval`, `gcs`, and `render` for one event and writes `event_report.md`, which lists what was measured, what was assumed and why, what data were missing, the fit quality for each instrument, and the figures (`corona3d/pipeline.py`). The shell is fitted freely first, and the fit with the direction held at the flare site follows when the free fit fails or is poor (mean IoU below `--iou-min`, default 0.5, or a parameter at a bound of the optimisation, `gcs.quality`). The default figure is drawn, with the coronagraph and radio layers when their data exist. Every step skips work that is done (`event_state.json`), and the command ends with exit code 1 when a step failed. The options are `--fit`, `--height-out`, `--height-note`, `--iou-min`, `--views`, `--t-ref`, `--radio-search`, `--home`, and `--force`.
- `corona3d radio-interval`, and the step of the same name in `corona3d event`, choose the start, end, and step of the radio blobs from the images, namely from the brightest source of each frequency above `min_tb` (`corona3d/radio.py`). The entries written by `corona3d new` carry `"interval_source": "default"` and are the only ones changed, so that an interval given by hand is kept.
- `corona3d fetch --nrh` downloads nothing, since the 2-D NRH images are supplied only on request by the Radio Solar Database at Nançay; it reports the frequencies of each NRH entry that the files in `data/nrh/` cover, and the manual step for the others. The 2013 October 25 render is unchanged and pixel-identical to the baseline.
- Offline tests of the order of the steps, of skipping finished work, of the fallback to the fixed-direction fit, of the report, and of the interval chosen from synthetic NRH images (`tests/test_event.py`).
- Python interface for analysis without the figure: `corona3d.build(home, params, **layers)` returns a `Reconstruction` with the density iso-surface (vertices, triangles, and the Alfvén speed and electron density at each vertex), the open, closed, and streamer field lines with their class, the GCS shell at the time of the reconstruction and of the coronagraph images, the coronagraph planes, a table of the radio source positions, and the observers, all in R⊙ in Heliographic Carrington Cartesian coordinates. The radio table also gives the sky-plane position seen from Earth, the height, and the Carrington and Stonyhurst longitude and latitude of every position. `corona3d.load_config` is exported, and `__all__` lists the public names (`corona3d/api.py`). The figure is built by the same functions and is unchanged (the 2013 October 25 render is pixel-identical to the one of the previous commit).
- `corona3d export [--format npz|csv|vtk] [--out DIR]` and `corona3d.export` write every enabled layer to data files (one npz archive, one table per layer, or VTK files for ParaView) together with `metadata.json`, which records the corona3d version, the SHA-256 and the values of the configuration, the model run, the event time, the coordinate frame and units, the observers, and the sources of every input (`corona3d/exporting.py`). Offline tests of the arrays, the three formats, and one round trip per format (`tests/test_api_export.py`).
- The preset `2024-05-09` holds the GCS shell fitted to COR1-A and COR2-A at 17:55 UT (apex 3.722 R⊙) and an assumed apex height of 1.77 R⊙ at 17:32 UT; the new optional key `gcs_note` records the reason, is ignored by the figure, and is copied into the metadata of the export.
- Configuration files written by version 0.1.0 and by the commits before this release load again: `stereo_b` is read as `stereo` with `stereo_sc` "B", views from `stereo_a` or `stereo_b` as views from `stereo`, and `radio_nrh` as an entry of `radio_images` (`paths.legacy`, `tests/test_legacy_config.py`).
- `corona3d gcs` fits the shell of the CME to the white-light images of the working directory and writes it into `config.json`. The fit of `scripts/gcs_fit.py` is now the module `corona3d/gcs.py` (the script is removed), and the command takes the same options (`--t-ref`, `--t-out`, `--flare LON LAT`, `--views`, `--fix-direction`, `--front`, `--skip-step1`) with the folder `data/wl` as default. It replaces the keys `gcs` and `gcs_wl`, writes `gcs_fit.json` beside them, and records in the new key `gcs_fit` the times, instruments, intersection over union per instrument, front speed, apex height with its uncertainty, and the parameters held fixed. `--running-diff MINUTES` builds the envelopes from running differences, and `--height-out` with `--height-note` gives the apex height at the time of the reconstruction by hand and records it as assumed. PyThea, SunPy, and scikit-image are imported only when a fit is run, and the tests of the pure functions and of the written keys use a mocked fit (`tests/test_gcs_fit.py`).
- The keys of `scripts/gcs_fit_2013-10-25.json` are `t_ref` and `t_out`, with their times, in place of `t1524` and `t1500`; the values are unchanged (1.931 R⊙, 1039 km s⁻¹).
- STEREO-A and STEREO-B are both supported: `stereo` and `stereo_sc` replace `stereo_b`, and the third panel, the COR1 and COR2 layers, and the download follow `stereo_sc`.
- Event presets: `corona3d init --event NAME` (the preset `2024-05-09` is added), and `corona3d new --time T --flare S14W28` writes `config.json` of a new event from Earth and STEREO positions, the PSI run of the Carrington rotation, and the image windows. User guide, Section 9, describes these and the fit.
- The radio layer also reads the spectral images of OVRO-LWA (`corona3d/ovro_lwa.py`; 32 to 87 MHz, from 2024 April onward), and `corona3d fetch --ovro-lwa` downloads them from the public OVRO-LWA solar data bucket. The radio image sets are listed under `radio_images` in `config.json`, one entry per instrument (NRH or OVRO-LWA), in place of `radio_nrh`; h5py is a new dependency. Offline tests on synthetic OVRO-LWA files (`tests/test_ovro_lwa.py`).
- The radio layer reads the 2-D images of the Nançay Radioheliograph (`corona3d/nrh.py`), copied into `data/nrh/` of the working directory, and draws the sources of a burst as a chain of coloured blobs for each frequency, one per time step, at the Newkirk height of that frequency, in the manner of the blobs in Fig. 7 of Morosan et al. (2022); contours of the last image are optional (`radio_nrh` in `config.json`). For 2013 October 25, the images at 150.9 and 432 MHz between 14:59 and 15:03 UT give five and three blobs.
- At most 150 streamer lines are drawn (`streamer_max_lines`), more faintly when the radio layer is on, so that the blobs remain visible; the notes of each panel are placed at its top right.
- `"projection": "perspective"` draws the panels with the perspective camera of the published Fig. 7 (`camera_distance` 6.65 R⊙), and the panels have its aspect (`window` 1200 × 918 pixels); the orthographic projection remains the default.
- The README compares the 2013 October 25 validation figure with Fig. 7 of Morosan et al. (2022) and states where the two differ.
- Offline tests of the NRH reader and of the radio blobs on synthetic NRH files (`tests/test_nrh.py`).
- The height-time step of `corona3d gcs` is divided into three functions (`projection_factor`, `front_profile`, and `extrapolate`), which `tests/test_gcs_fit.py` checks on a synthetic rise with a known speed and height; the fit of 2013 October 25 is unchanged (1.931 R⊙, 1039 km s⁻¹), and CI runs these tests from the checkout.
- The installation and the offline tests were checked on Python 3.12.15 as well as 3.13.
- The README, METHODS.md, the user guide, the model audit, and this changelog were edited into the author's writing style, with every number, citation, and command unchanged.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-27)

A review of version 0.0.1 found errors in the physics, the code, the documentation, and the packaging, and this version corrects all of them (`docs/MODEL_AUDIT.md`, items F1 to F9).

- The model is no longer rotated by −8.243° in Carrington longitude (`model_dlon` = 0), since MAS is a steady-state Carrington model and the observers' longitudes already contain the solar rotation.
- The panels are drawn with an orthographic projection, so that the model, the shell, the radio sources, and the coronagraph images share one scale, whereas the perspective camera had magnified structures in front of the plane of the sky by up to 18 %.
- The CME shell at 15:00 UT keeps the shape of the 15:24 UT white-light fit, and its apex height (1.931 ± 0.158 R⊙) is extrapolated from the COR1-B front, which moves at 1039 km s⁻¹. The shell at 15:24 UT is drawn when coronagraph images are shown, and the fit is reproducible with `corona3d gcs`, with its result kept in `scripts/gcs_fit_2013-10-25.json`.
- The streamer loops are now the closed field lines through the CME shell, selected without free parameters, in place of a tuned seed line.
- Field lines are traced through the whole model (to 30.5 R⊙) with a step of 0.01 r and classed as open, closed, or undetermined, and they are drawn within 3 R⊙ (`line_r_max`).
- The viewpoint of every panel can be chosen (`views` in `config.json`, `--view` on the command line) as Earth, STEREO-B, the north pole, or any Carrington longitude and latitude with a roll angle, and the panel images are now `view_a.png`, `view_b.png`, and `view_c.png`.
- `va_density` chooses the density from which the Alfvén speed is computed. Moreover, radio positions in arcsec are converted with the angular solar radius at the time of the event, and a position that cannot lie at its height produces a warning.
- Coronagraph images are compared with the first frame of `wl_window` as a ratio by default (`wl_mode`), which removes vignetting. In addition, inputs that would draw a wrong or blank image are refused, COR1 triplets are grouped by time, frames of different size are resampled, times with a time zone are accepted, and each panel states the times of the model, the shell, and the images.
- Nested configuration keys are merged one by one, `make_figure` reads the working directory's `config.json` and writes inside that directory, and `reconstruct` accepts partial parameters.
- Damaged and partial downloads are removed, `corona3d fetch` ends with exit code 1 when files fail, and `--cor1a` adds the STEREO-A COR1 images for the GCS fit.
- Python 3.12 or newer is required by PyThea 1.3, the dependencies have lower bounds, and PyThea is pinned to 1.3. The version now has one source, a release workflow was added, and CI installs HDF4 on macOS and runs the authorship tests.
- The command line no longer loads VTK for `--version` and `init`, and figures are drawn without a GUI backend.
- Offline tests were added for the MAS units and the Alfvén speed, the model rotation, the streamer selection, the undetermined lines, the radio warning, the coronagraph frames (five roll angles, ratio and difference, mixed sizes, refused inputs, C2 headers, and COR1 triplets across a minute), the configuration merge, the panel scale, the viewpoints, and the downloads, giving 40 offline tests in all.
- The README, METHODS.md, and the user guide were corrected against the code, and `docs/MODEL_AUDIT.md` was added.

## 0.0.1 (2026-10-27)

First pre-release, in a private repository.

- Three-dimensional reconstruction of a coronal mass ejection and the surrounding corona in the layout of Fig. 7 of Morosan et al. (2022), seen from Earth, from above the solar north pole, and from STEREO-B, built from public data only, with every layer switchable from the command line (`--<layer>` / `--no-<layer>`) and from Python (`make_figure(..., layer=True)`).
- Layers: the electron-density surface of a PSI MAS/MAST run at a chosen plasma level, coloured by the Alfvén speed computed from the scaled density; open and closed field lines; the streamer loops above the CME flank, from a seed line; the 1.2 R⊙ reference sphere; the GCS shell of the CME (PyThea); radio sources placed at their four-fold Newkirk harmonic heights from sky-plane positions given by the user; and base-difference images of LASCO C2 and of STEREO-B COR1 (total brightness of the polarisation triplets) and COR2, drawn in the plane of the sky of their spacecraft.
- A working directory with its own `config.json` (`corona3d init`), downloads of the model run from the PSI archive and of the coronagraph images through the Virtual Solar Observatory (`corona3d fetch`), and figures with their full-resolution panels (`corona3d render`).
- Field-line tracer written for corona3d (fourth-order Runge-Kutta along the unit field vector), since the tracer of psipy did not follow these files correctly.
- GCS parameters of the event fitted again from public images, as the paper does not list them (METHODS.md, Section 7).
- Offline tests: the tracer against the analytic field lines of a dipole, the radio heights of the paper, the observer axes and the deprojection, the placement of a feature from synthetic coronagraph images at three roll angles, the grouping of COR1 triplets, the command line, and a rendering without data; an online test that downloads the data of 2013 October 25 and draws both figures of the README (`CORONA3D_ONLINE=1`).
- README, user guide (`docs/USER_GUIDE.md`), and METHODS.md, with every reference checked against its registry record (`docs/refs.bib`).
